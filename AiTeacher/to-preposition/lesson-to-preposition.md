# "To" Beyond Verbs — Preposition, Direction, and Indirect Objects

A structured grammar lesson on `to` before nouns, pronouns, and `-ing` — not as an infinitive marker.

**Level:** B1–C1  
**Focus:** grammar, prepositions, accuracy  
**Learner context:** CS, engineering, university applications (ZJU)

**Related lesson:** [verb-to-patterns/lesson-verb-to-patterns.md](../verb-to-patterns/lesson-verb-to-patterns.md)  
**Practice:** [exercises-to-preposition.md](./exercises-to-preposition.md)

---

## Core Idea

`to` is **not only** a bridge to the next verb (`want to go`).

It is also one of the most common **prepositions** in English — it connects words to **nouns, pronouns, and gerunds**.

```
to + verb      → infinitive or purpose     "want to go" / "came to study"
to + noun/pron → preposition               "go to school" / "talk to me"
to + -ing      → preposition + gerund      "look forward to meeting"
```

### Quick test

Look at the word **after** `to`:

| After `to` | Type | Example |
|------------|------|---------|
| base verb | infinitive / purpose | want **to apply** |
| me / him / Shanghai / the lab | preposition | send it **to me** |
| meeting / working | preposition + gerund | look forward **to starting** |

## 1. Direction and Destination — `to + place`

`to` answers **where?** (movement toward a place).

### Rule

Use `to` with verbs of movement and transfer when you name a **destination**.

### Common verbs

`go`, `move`, `travel`, `fly`, `walk`, `run`, `drive`, `send`, `return`, `come`

### Examples

1. I **moved to** Shanghai for my language year.
2. She **went to** the admissions office.
3. We **flew to** Beijing for the conference.
4. He **walked to** the lab after class.
5. They **sent the documents to** Zhejiang University.

### Memory tip

**Movement + destination → to.**  
*Where? → to the place.*

---

## 2. Indirect Object — `to + person` (whom?)

`to` marks **who receives** something — the indirect object.

### Rule

Pattern: **verb + thing + to + person**

Many verbs allow two structures:

| Pattern A | Pattern B |
|-----------|-----------|
| give **the professor** the draft | give **the draft to the professor** |
| send **me** the file | send **the file to me** |
| show **us** the results | show **the results to us** |

### Pronouns after `to`

`to me`, `to you`, `to him`, `to her`, `to us`, `to them`

### Common verbs

`give`, `send`, `show`, `tell`, `explain`, `offer`, `pass`, `write`, `read`, `lend`, `return`

### Examples

1. I **sent the application to** the admissions team.
2. Can you **explain this to me** in simpler terms?
3. She **showed the prototype to** the investors.
4. He **wrote an email to** his supervisor.
5. They **offered a scholarship to** three students.

### Memory tip

**Give/send/show something TO someone.**

---

## 3. Communication — `to + person` (with whom?)

Some verbs use `to` to mark **the listener / partner**, not a destination.

### Common verbs

`talk`, `speak`, `listen`, `apologize`, `reply`, `write`, `shout`, `whisper`

### Examples

1. I need **to talk to** the program coordinator.
2. She **spoke to** the professor after the lecture.
3. **Listen to me** for a second.
4. He **apologized to** the team for the delay.
5. Please **reply to** my email by Friday.

### Contrast: speak **with** vs speak **to**

- speak **to** someone — often one-directional (lecture, announcement)
- speak **with** someone — two-way conversation (both sides talk)

Both are correct; `with` sounds more conversational.

## 4. Relation and Belonging — `to + noun`

`to` links two things in a **logical or possessive** relationship.

### Common patterns

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| key **to** | key for opening | the key **to** the server room |
| answer **to** | solution of | the answer **to** the problem |
| solution **to** | fix for | a solution **to** the bug |
| belong **to** | be part of | this repo **belongs to** the team |
| married **to** | spouse | married **to** an engineer |
| similar **to** | like | similar **to** our approach |
| important **to** | matters for | this is important **to** me |
| related **to** | connected with | issues related **to** latency |
| according **to** | based on | according **to** the guidelines |

### Examples

1. What is the best solution **to** this performance issue?
2. This module **belongs to** the core architecture.
3. Your background is very relevant **to** the CS program.
4. **According to** the syllabus, the exam is in June.
5. His approach is similar **to** mine.

### Memory tip

**A to B** = A is connected / directed / related **toward** B.

---

## 5. Comparison — `prefer X to Y`

`to` can mean **instead of / rather than** in comparisons.

### Rule

`prefer` + noun/gerund + **to** + noun/gerund

### Examples

1. I **prefer working in** small teams **to working in** huge corporations.
2. She **prefers Python to** Java for prototyping.
3. I **prefer walking to** taking the metro when the weather is good.

### Memory tip

**prefer A to B** — not *prefer A than B*.

---

## 6. Time — `minutes to the hour`

`to` can mean **before** a full hour.

### Examples

- ten **to** five = 4:50
- quarter **to** six = 5:45
- twenty **to** three = 2:40

### Memory tip

**X to the hour** = X minutes **before** that hour.

---

## 7. Fixed Phrases with `to + noun/pronoun`

### Common phrases

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| thanks **to** | because of (positive) | Thanks **to** your help, I passed. |
| next **to** | beside | Sit **next to** me. |
| close **to** | near | The campus is close **to** the city center. |
| in addition **to** | besides | In addition **to** CS, I study math. |
| due **to** | because of (formal) | The delay was due **to** a network error. |
| opposed **to** | against | I am opposed **to** that approach. |

### Examples

1. **Thanks to** the tutorial, I understood the API.
2. The FabLab is close **to** the engineering building.
3. **In addition to** algorithms, the program covers systems design.
4. The outage was due **to** a misconfigured deployment.
5. She sat next **to** the team lead during the review.

## 8. `to` as Preposition + `-ing`

When `to` is a **preposition**, the next verb must be **`-ing`**, not a base verb.

### Rule

If you can replace the verb with a **noun**, `to` is a preposition → use **`-ing`**.

### Common phrases

`look forward to`, `be used to`, `get used to`, `object to`, `admit to`, `devote ... to`, `be opposed to`, `in addition to`

### Examples

1. I **look forward to starting** my degree at ZJU.
2. He **is used to working** under tight deadlines.
3. She **objected to being** reassigned without notice.
4. I **admit to underestimating** the scope of the project.
5. We are **devoted to building** reliable systems.

### Test

- look forward to **the meeting** → look forward to **meeting**
- used to **the schedule** → used to **working** on that schedule

### Memory tip

**Noun fits after `to`? → verb becomes `-ing`.**

---

## 9. Adjective + `to` + Infinitive

Some adjectives take **`to + verb`** (infinitive), not a noun.

### Common adjectives

`glad`, `happy`, `ready`, `willing`, `able`, `likely`, `easy`, `hard`, `difficult`, `important` (in some patterns)

### Examples

1. I am **glad to meet** you.
2. She is **ready to deploy** the update.
3. This algorithm is **easy to understand**.
4. He was **willing to help** with the integration.
5. The system is **likely to fail** under heavy load.

### Contrast

- important **to** me *(preposition + pronoun)*
- important **to understand** *(infinitive after adjective)*

---

## 10. Introductory `to` Phrases

`to` starts many fixed expressions — not followed by a verb at all.

### Examples

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **to** be honest | honestly speaking |
| **to** my surprise | surprisingly for me |
| **to** some extent | partly |
| **to** tell the truth | frankly |

1. **To be honest**, the lecture was harder than I expected.
2. **To my surprise**, the interview went well.
3. I agree with you **to some extent**.

## 11. Quick Decision Flowchart

```
You see "to"
│
├─ Next word is a base verb?
│   ├─ After want/decide/hope? → infinitive (verb-to-patterns §1)
│   ├─ Answers "why?" / "in order to"? → purpose (verb-to-patterns §5)
│   └─ After glad/ready/easy? → adjective + infinitive (§9)
│
├─ Next word is me/him/Shanghai/the door? → preposition (§1–§4)
│
├─ Next word is -ing? → preposition (§8)
│
└─ Fixed phrase (according to, thanks to)? → learn as chunk (§7, §10)
```

## 12. Common Mistakes (and Fixes)

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|-------|-------|-----|
| go in Shanghai | go **to** Shanghai | destination needs `to` |
| give me it | give **it to** me / give me **it** | indirect object order |
| listen me | listen **to** me | `listen` needs `to` |
| prefer A than B | prefer A **to** B | comparison uses `to` |
| look forward to meet | look forward to **meeting** | `to` = preposition here |
| similar with this | similar **to** this | fixed collocation |
| explain me the code | explain **the code to** me | explain needs `to` for person |
| married with her | married **to** her | married + to |
