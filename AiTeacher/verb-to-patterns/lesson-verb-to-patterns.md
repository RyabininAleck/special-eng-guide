# "To" Between Verbs and After Verbs

A structured grammar lesson on when to use `to`, bare infinitive, or `-ing` after verbs.

**Level:** B1–C1  
**Focus:** grammar, accuracy, academic/professional English  
**Learner context:** CS, engineering, university applications (ZJU)

**Related lesson:** [to-preposition/lesson-to-preposition.md](../to-preposition/lesson-to-preposition.md)  
**Practice:** [exercises-verb-to-patterns.md](./exercises-verb-to-patterns.md)

---

## Core Idea

English does **not** allow two full verbs next to each other without a connector.

- Wrong: `I want go.`
- Right: `I want to go.`

After most verbs, the next verb needs a form marker: **`to + verb`**, **bare verb**, or **`-ing`**.

---

## 1. The To-Infinitive: `verb (+ object) + to + verb`

Many verbs expressing **intention, desire, decision, plan, attempt, obligation, or instruction** take **`to + base verb`**.

Same rule — two shapes:

| Pattern | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **A** | verb + to + verb | I want **to apply**. |
| **B** | verb + object + to + verb | The professor **asked me to revise** it. |

Pattern B adds **who** performs the action. The verbs below are one family.

### Common verbs

**Both patterns** (with or without object):

`want`, `expect`, `need`, `would like`, `prefer`

**Usually Pattern A** (no object before `to`):

`decide`, `plan`, `hope`, `agree`, `refuse`, `promise`, `attempt`, `manage`, `fail`, `offer`, `choose`, `learn`, `prepare`, `afford`, `pretend`, `seem`, `appear`

**Usually Pattern B** (object required):

`ask`, `tell`, `encourage`, `allow`, `invite`, `remind`, `teach`, `advise`, `force`, `persuade`, `require`

### Examples — Pattern A

1. I **want to apply** to Zhejiang University.
2. We **decided to refactor** the telemetry module.
3. She **needs to improve** her academic writing.
4. They **failed to detect** the anomaly in the dataset.
5. He **managed to finish** the firmware update before the race.

### Examples — Pattern B

1. The professor **asked me to revise** the introduction.
2. They **encouraged us to participate** in the hackathon.
3. The rules **require students to maintain** a GPA above 3.0.
4. I **reminded him to back up** the database.
5. I **want you to review** the pull request before merging.

### Exception — `let` / `make` + object (no `to`)

These take an object but **not** `to`:

- They **let me join** the robotics team.
- The deadline **made us prioritize** core features.

### Memory tip

**One rule: intention / instruction → `to`.**  
No object → `want to go`. With object → `want him to go`.

## 2. When NO `to` Is Used: Bare Infinitive

Sometimes the second verb appears **without `to`**.

### Rule A — After modal verbs

`can`, `could`, `will`, `would`, `should`, `may`, `might`, `must`

- I **can explain** the algorithm clearly.
- You **should practice** speaking every day.
- We **must submit** the application before the deadline.

No `to` after modals.

### Rule B — After causative / permissive verbs

`let`, `make`

- The supervisor **let me present** the research proposal.
- The bug **made the system crash**.

### Rule C — After perception verbs (complete action witnessed)

`see`, `hear`, `watch`, `feel`, `notice` + **object + bare verb**

- I **saw the car stop** suddenly.
- We **heard the engine fail** during testing.

If you describe an **ongoing** action, use **`-ing`**:

- I **saw him working** on the PCB layout.

### Memory tip

**Modals and make / let = no `to`.**

---

## 3. `verb + -ing` Instead of `to`

Some verbs take **`-ing`**, not `to`.

### Rule

Verbs about **enjoyment, continuation, avoiding, or minding** often take `-ing`.

### Common verbs

`enjoy`, `avoid`, `finish`, `keep`, `mind`, `suggest`, `recommend`, `consider`, `miss`, `practice`, `deny`, `admit`, `risk`

### Examples

1. I **enjoy solving** algorithmic problems.
2. He **avoided mentioning** his previous employer.
3. We **finished implementing** the API.
4. She **suggested applying** for a research internship.
5. I **practice speaking** English daily.

### Memory tip

**Enjoy / avoid / finish → -ing.**

---

## 4. Verbs That Take Both — With Different Meanings

| Verb | + **-ing** | + **to** |
|------|------------|----------|
| **stop** | stop doing = quit an activity | stop to do = pause in order to do something else |
| **remember** | remember doing = recall a past action | remember to do = not forget a future duty |
| **forget** | forget doing = no memory of past action | forget to do = fail to do something |
| **try** | try doing = experiment with a method | try to do = attempt / make effort |
| **regret** | regret doing = feel sorry about past | regret to say/inform = formal announcement |
| **go on** | go on doing = continue same thing | go on to do = move to next stage |

### Examples

1. I **stopped working** at the bank. *(I quit.)*
2. I **stopped to check** the logs. *(I paused in order to check.)*
3. I **remember meeting** the professor in Beijing.
4. **Remember to submit** your motivation letter.
5. **Try restarting** the service. *(method)*
6. I **tried to fix** the bug all night. *(effort)*

### Memory tip

**-ing = real / past / method.**  
**to = duty / goal / effort.**

---

## 5. `to` for Purpose

`to + verb` can mean **in order to** — not always governed by the previous verb.

- I moved to Shanghai **to study** computer science.
- We optimized the code **to reduce** latency.
- He attended the lecture **to learn** about forecasting models.

---

## 6. When `to` Is a Preposition → Use `-ing`

If `to` is a **preposition** (not part of an infinitive), the next verb must be **`-ing`**.

### Common phrases

`look forward to`, `be used to`, `get used to`, `object to`, `admit to`, `devote ... to`, `prefer ... to`, `in addition to`

### Examples

1. I **look forward to starting** my degree.
2. He **is used to working** under pressure.
3. She **objected to being assigned** a new task.
4. I **admit to underestimating** the complexity.
5. We are **devoted to building** reliable systems.

### Test

Can you insert a noun?

- look forward to **the meeting** → so: look forward to **meeting**

### Memory tip

If `to` can be replaced by a noun, the verb becomes **`-ing`**.

## 7. Quick Decision Flowchart

```
Second verb needed?
│
├─ After modal (can/must/should)? → bare verb
│     "I should go"
│
├─ After let/make + object? → bare verb
│     "let me go"
│
├─ After enjoy/avoid/finish? → -ing
│     "enjoy learning"
│
├─ After like/hate/start? → -ing OR to (similar meaning)
│     "like coding" / "like to code"
│
├─ After stop/remember/try? → -ing OR to (different meaning!)
│     "stopped working" vs "stopped to work"
│
├─ After help? → bare verb OR to
│     "help me fix" / "help me to fix"
│
├─ After want/decide/ask/tell? → to + verb
│     "want to learn" / "ask me to help"
│
├─ "to" is preposition (look forward to)? → -ing
│     "look forward to meeting"
│
└─ Purpose? → to + verb
      "came here to study"
```

## 8. Common Mistakes (and Fixes)

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|-------|-------|-----|
| I want go | I want **to** go | `want` needs infinitive |
| I must to submit | I must submit | no `to` after modals |
| I enjoy to code | I enjoy **coding** | `enjoy` → -ing |
| I look forward to meet you | look forward to **meeting** you | `to` = preposition |
| He made me to wait | made me **wait** | causative = bare verb |
| I stopped to working | stopped **working** OR stopped **to work** | choose meaning carefully |
