# Physics Vocabulary Lesson 1

## Topic

Measurement, units, mass, volume, and density

## Goal

In this first lesson, you build the foundation for speaking about physical quantities in English. You learn what these core words mean, how they connect to each other, and how to use them in short scientific sentences.

## Main Rule

In physics, we describe reality with a **quantity + number + unit**.

For example:
- `mass = 2 kg`
- `volume = 500 cm^3`
- `density = 7.8 g/cm^3`

Without a unit, the number is usually incomplete. Without clear measurement, the statement is too vague.

## 1. Measurement

### Brief Explanation

A `measurement` is the process of finding the value of a physical quantity.

In physics, measurement turns observation into precise information. Instead of saying "the object is heavy," we say "the mass is 4 kilograms."

### Examples

1. Accurate measurement is necessary in every physics experiment.
2. The measurement of the wire length was repeated three times.
3. Temperature measurement helps engineers monitor system safety.
4. A poor measurement can lead to a wrong conclusion.

## 2. Unit

### Brief Explanation

A `unit` is the standard used to express a measurement.

Common examples are `meter`, `second`, `kilogram`, and `liter`. A number without a unit often has no practical meaning in science.

### Examples

1. The kilogram is the SI unit of mass.
2. Always write the unit after the numerical value.
3. Seconds are the standard unit for time.
4. The result is incomplete because the unit is missing.

## 3. Mass

### Brief Explanation

`Mass` is the amount of matter in an object.

Mass is usually measured in kilograms or grams. In basic physics, mass is different from weight: weight depends on gravity, while mass does not.

### Examples

1. The mass of the metal block is 2 kilograms.
2. A larger mass usually requires more force to accelerate.
3. We measured the mass of the sample before heating it.
4. The object has small volume but large mass.

## 4. Volume

### Brief Explanation

`Volume` is the amount of space that an object or substance occupies.

For solids, liquids, and gases, volume tells us how much space they take up. It can be expressed in cubic meters, cubic centimeters, or liters.

### Examples

1. The volume of the container is 1 liter.
2. Gas expands to fill the available volume.
3. We calculated the volume of the cube from its side length.
4. The same mass can have a different volume in a different form.

## 5. Density

### Brief Explanation

`Density` is mass per unit volume.

Density helps us compare materials. If two objects have the same volume, the one with greater mass has greater density.

Formula:

`density = mass / volume`

### Examples

1. Aluminum has lower density than copper.
2. Oil floats on water because its density is lower.
3. We can calculate density from mass and volume.
4. High density means a lot of mass in a small space.

## How The Ideas Connect

- `measurement` is the process.
- `unit` is the standard language of the result.
- `mass` tells us how much matter an object has.
- `volume` tells us how much space it occupies.
- `density` connects mass and volume.

Simple comparison:

- large mass + small volume -> high density
- small mass + large volume -> low density

## Useful Language Patterns

These sentence patterns are common in academic English:

1. `The mass of the object is ...`
2. `The volume of the sample is ...`
3. `The measurement was taken with ...`
4. `The unit of mass is ...`
5. `The density depends on mass and volume.`

## Suggested Speaking Prompt

Answer aloud:

"In physics, measurement is important because we need precise values. Mass tells us how much matter an object has, volume tells us how much space it occupies, and density shows the relation between them."

## Physics Calculation Practice

Use the formula:

`density = mass / volume`

Read the formula aloud:

`Density equals mass divided by volume.`

### Exercise 1

An object has a mass of `20 g` and a volume of `10 cm^3`.

Calculate the density.

Answer pattern:

`The density is ... g/cm^3.`

### Exercise 2

A metal block has a mass of `300 g` and a volume of `100 cm^3`.

Calculate the density.

Answer pattern:

`The density of the metal block is ... g/cm^3.`

### Exercise 3

A liquid has a mass of `500 g` and a volume of `250 cm^3`.

Calculate the density.

Then answer:

`Is this liquid denser than water if water has a density of 1 g/cm^3?`

## Teacher Notes: Plan Check

This is a strong first lesson for the physics plan because it starts with the most universal scientific language. It creates the base needed for later topics such as motion, force, pressure, and energy.

One recommendation: after this lesson, it is useful to add a short bridge lesson or exercise block on **reading formulas and units aloud in English**, because the learner will immediately need forms such as:

- `kg`
- `m^3`
- `g/cm^3`
- `density = mass / volume`
