# Physics Vocabulary Lesson 4

## Topic

Force, mass, acceleration, and Newton's second law

## Goal

In this lesson, you learn how to speak about why motion changes. Lesson 3 explained motion, speed, velocity, and acceleration. This lesson explains the physical cause behind acceleration: force.

## Main Rule

In basic mechanics, force changes motion.

The central relationship is:

`force = mass * acceleration`

Read it aloud:

`Force equals mass times acceleration.`

This equation is called `Newton's second law`.

It means:

- a larger force causes larger acceleration,
- a larger mass is harder to accelerate,
- acceleration depends on both force and mass.

## 1. Force

### Brief Explanation

`Force` is a push or pull that can change the motion of an object.

If you push a box, pull a rope, press a button, or lift a tool, you apply force. In physics, force is not just effort. It is a measurable interaction between objects. A force can make an object speed up, slow down, start moving, stop moving, or change direction.

Force is measured in `newtons`, written as `N`. One newton is the force needed to accelerate a mass of one kilogram at one meter per second squared.

In English, we often use the verbs `apply`, `exert`, and `produce` with force:

- `apply a force`
- `exert a force`
- `produce a force`

### Examples

1. A force can change the motion of an object.
2. The motor applies a force to the wheel.
3. Gravity exerts a force on the falling object.
4. A greater force can produce greater acceleration.

## 2. Mass

### Brief Explanation

`Mass` is the amount of matter in an object.

You already learned this word in Lesson 1, but now we use it in mechanics. Mass is important because it affects how difficult it is to change an object's motion. A heavy truck needs more force to accelerate than a small bicycle because the truck has greater mass.

Mass is measured in `kilograms` (`kg`) in the SI system. In Newton's second law, mass is one of the two main factors that determine acceleration.

Important idea:

`larger mass -> smaller acceleration if the force is the same`

### Examples

1. The mass of the object is 5 kilograms.
2. Larger mass requires larger force for the same acceleration.
3. The acceleration depends on the mass of the object.
4. A small mass is easier to accelerate than a large mass.

## 3. Acceleration

### Brief Explanation

`Acceleration` is a change in velocity over time.

In this lesson, acceleration is the result of force acting on mass. If you apply a force to an object, the object may accelerate. How much it accelerates depends on the size of the force and the mass of the object.

Acceleration is measured in `meters per second squared`, written as `m/s^2`. This unit means that velocity changes by a certain number of meters per second every second.

Example:

If acceleration is `3 m/s^2`, the velocity increases by `3 m/s` every second, if the acceleration stays constant.

### Examples

1. Acceleration increases when force increases.
2. Acceleration decreases when mass increases.
3. The object has an acceleration of 2 meters per second squared.
4. Newton's second law connects force, mass, and acceleration.

## 4. Newton's Second Law

### Brief Explanation

`Newton's second law` describes the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration.

The formula is:

`F = m * a`

In words:

`force equals mass times acceleration`

This law is one of the most important ideas in mechanics. It helps us calculate how much force is needed to move an object, how much acceleration a force will produce, or how much mass an object has if force and acceleration are known.

For engineering, this law is practical. It helps with vehicle design, robotics, motors, braking systems, elevators, drones, and mechanical structures.

### Examples

1. Newton's second law is written as `F = m * a`.
2. The equation relates force, mass, and acceleration.
3. If mass is constant, greater force produces greater acceleration.
4. Engineers use Newton's second law to analyze motion.

## How The Ideas Connect

- `force` is the push or pull.
- `mass` tells us how much matter the object has.
- `acceleration` tells us how velocity changes.
- `Newton's second law` connects them in one equation.

Simple comparison:

- same mass + greater force -> greater acceleration
- same force + greater mass -> smaller acceleration
- same acceleration + greater mass -> greater force needed

## Useful Language Patterns

These sentence patterns are common in physics English:

1. `A force acts on the object.`
2. `The object has a mass of ...`
3. `The acceleration is ...`
4. `Force equals mass times acceleration.`
5. `The force increases when acceleration increases.`
6. `A larger mass requires a larger force.`

## Common Confusion

### Force vs Energy

`Force` is a push or pull. `Energy` is the ability to do work or cause change.

Examples:

1. A hand applies force to a box.
2. A moving car has kinetic energy.
3. Force can transfer energy, but force and energy are not the same thing.

### Mass vs Weight

`Mass` is the amount of matter in an object. `Weight` is the force of gravity on that mass.

Examples:

1. Mass is measured in kilograms.
2. Weight is measured in newtons.
3. Your mass is almost the same on Earth and the Moon.
4. Your weight is different on Earth and the Moon because gravity is different.
