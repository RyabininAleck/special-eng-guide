# Physics Vocabulary Lesson 5

## Topic

Work, energy, power, kinetic energy, and potential energy

## Goal

In this lesson, you learn the vocabulary for describing physical change through energy. You already know that force can change motion. Now you learn what happens when a force moves an object through a distance: in physics, this is called work.

## Main Rule

In physics, `work` is done when a force moves an object through a distance.

Basic formula:

`work = force * distance`

Read it aloud:

`Work equals force times distance.`

Energy and work are closely connected:

- `work` is energy transferred by force,
- `energy` is the ability to do work,
- `power` tells us how quickly work is done or energy is transferred.

## 1. Work

### Brief Explanation

`Work` has a special meaning in physics. It does not simply mean effort, labor, or a job. In physics, work is done only when a force causes movement in the direction of the force.

For example, if you push a box and the box moves, you do work on the box. If you push a wall and the wall does not move, you use effort, but in basic physics you do no mechanical work on the wall because there is no distance moved.

Work depends on two things: force and distance. A larger force or a larger distance usually means more work.

Work is measured in `joules`, written as `J`.

### Visual: Work = Force × Distance

```
      Force (F)
        ↓
   ┌─────────┐
   │  Object  │
   └─────────┘
        ↓
        ↓  Distance (d)
        ↓
   ──────────────►
```

Work is done when force causes displacement.

### Examples

1. Work is done when a force moves an object.
2. The motor does work on the elevator.
3. No mechanical work is done if the object does not move.
4. Work is measured in joules.

## 2. Energy

### Brief Explanation

`Energy` is the ability to do work or cause change.

Energy is one of the most important ideas in physics because it appears in almost every topic: mechanics, heat, electricity, waves, light, and modern physics. Energy can move from one object to another, and it can change form.

For example, a moving car has kinetic energy. A battery stores chemical energy. Hot water has thermal energy. A raised object has potential energy because gravity can make it fall.

Energy is also measured in `joules` (`J`). This is important: work and energy use the same unit because work is a way of transferring energy.

### Visual: Energy Forms

```
Energy
├── Kinetic (motion)
├── Potential (stored)
│   ├── Gravitational (height)
│   ├── Elastic (spring)
│   └── Chemical (battery)
├── Thermal (heat)
└── Electrical (current)
```

### Examples

1. Energy is the ability to do work.
2. A moving object has energy.
3. Energy can change from one form to another.
4. The battery stores energy for the circuit.

## 3. Kinetic Energy

### Brief Explanation

`Kinetic energy` is the energy of motion.

Any moving object has kinetic energy: a car, a drone, a falling stone, a robot arm, or an electron moving through a wire. If the object moves faster, its kinetic energy increases.

Kinetic energy depends on mass and speed. A heavy truck moving slowly can have a lot of kinetic energy because its mass is large. A small bullet can also have a lot of kinetic energy because its speed is very high.

You do not need the full formula yet, but you should remember this idea:

`more mass or more speed -> more kinetic energy`

### Visual: Kinetic Energy

```
   Speed ↑ → KE ↑
   Mass  ↑ → KE ↑

   Example: Car vs. Bicycle
   ┌───────┐   ┌─────────┐
   │ Car   │   │ Bicycle │
   │ heavy │   │ light   │
   │ fast  │   │ slow    │
   │ high KE│   │ low KE  │
   └───────┘   └─────────┘
```

### Examples

1. A moving car has kinetic energy.
2. Kinetic energy increases when speed increases.
3. A heavier object can have more kinetic energy.
4. The robot arm has kinetic energy while it is moving.

## 4. Potential Energy

### Brief Explanation

`Potential energy` is stored energy because of position, shape, or condition.

The easiest example is gravitational potential energy. If you lift an object, it stores energy because gravity can pull it down later. A book on a high shelf has more gravitational potential energy than the same book on the floor.

Another example is elastic potential energy. A compressed spring or stretched rubber band stores energy because it can return to its original shape and cause motion.

Potential energy is important because it helps us explain systems before motion happens. Something may look still, but it can still store energy.

### Visual: Gravitational Potential Energy

```
   Height (h)
      ↑
      │   ┌─────┐
      │   │Book │  High PE
      │   └─────┘
      │
      │   ┌─────┐
      │   │Book │  Low PE
      │   └─────┘
      └─────────────►
```

### Examples

1. A raised object has gravitational potential energy.
2. A compressed spring stores potential energy.
3. Potential energy can become kinetic energy.
4. The system stores energy before it starts moving.

## 5. Power

### Brief Explanation

`Power` tells us how quickly work is done or how quickly energy is transferred.

Two machines may do the same amount of work, but one machine may do it faster. The faster machine has greater power. This is why power is very important in engineering: motors, engines, batteries, computers, and electrical systems all have power limits.

Basic formula:

`power = work / time`

Read it aloud:

`Power equals work divided by time.`

Power is measured in `watts`, written as `W`. One watt means one joule per second.

### Visual: Power = Work / Time

```
   Work (J)     Time (s)     Power (W)
      │            │            │
      └──────┬─────┘            │
             │                  │
             ▼                  │
        Power = Work / Time ◄───┘
```

### Examples

1. Power describes how fast work is done.
2. A stronger motor can have greater power.
3. Power is measured in watts.
4. The device consumes 60 watts of power.

## 6. Joule And Watt

### Brief Explanation

A `joule` is the unit of work and energy.

A `watt` is the unit of power.

These two units are connected:

`1 watt = 1 joule per second`

This means that if a machine transfers 100 joules of energy every second, its power is 100 watts.

In technical English, be careful with the preposition `per`. We say:

- `joules per second`
- `meters per second`
- `newtons per square meter`

### Visual: Watt = Joule per Second

```
   1 Watt = 1 Joule / 1 Second

   Example:
   Power = 60 W
   Energy per second = 60 J
   Time = 1 s
   Energy transferred = 60 J
```

### Examples

1. Work is measured in joules.
2. Energy is measured in joules.
3. Power is measured in watts.
4. One watt is one joule per second.

## How The Ideas Connect

- `force` can move an object through a distance.
- when force causes movement, `work` is done.
- `work` transfers `energy`.
- moving objects have `kinetic energy`.
- raised or compressed objects can have `potential energy`.
- `power` tells us how quickly work is done.

### Visual: Connection Chain

```
Force → Movement → Work → Energy Transfer → Power
  │         │         │           │              │
  ▼         ▼         ▼           ▼              ▼
push    distance   F×d        joules         J/s = W
```

### Visual: Energy Transformation

```
Potential Energy (raised object)
        │
        │  gravity
        ▼
Kinetic Energy (falling object)
        │
        │  impact
        ▼
Heat + Sound Energy
```

## Useful Language Patterns

These sentence patterns are common in physics English:

1. `Work is done when a force moves an object.`
2. `Energy is the ability to do work.`
3. `Kinetic energy is energy of motion.`
4. `Potential energy is stored energy.`
5. `Power equals work divided by time.`
6. `The unit of energy is the joule.`
7. `The unit of power is the watt.`

## Common Confusion

### Work In Everyday English vs Work In Physics

In everyday English, `work` can mean a job or effort. In physics, `work` means energy transferred when force moves an object through a distance.

### Visual: Everyday vs Physics

```
Everyday: "I have a lot of work today."
Physics:  "The force does work on the object."
          (only if distance > 0)
```

Examples:

1. Everyday English: `I have a lot of work today.`
2. Physics English: `The force does work on the object.`
3. If the object does not move, mechanical work is zero in the simplest case.

### Energy vs Power

`Energy` is the amount of ability to do work. `Power` is how fast energy is used or transferred.

### Visual: Energy vs Power

```
   Energy: How much?
   Power:  How fast?

   Battery: stores energy (J)
   Device: uses power (W)
```

Examples:

1. A battery stores energy.
2. A device uses power.
3. A high-power motor transfers energy quickly.
4. Two machines can use the same energy, but the faster one has greater power.

```

Formula: W = F × d
Calculation: W = 10 × 5 = 50 J
Answer: The work done is 50 J.
```
