# Physics Vocabulary Lesson 6

## Topic

Temperature, heat, thermal energy, internal energy, and heat transfer

## Goal

In this lesson, you learn the vocabulary for describing thermal energy and how it moves. You already know that energy can be transferred by work. Now you learn about energy transfer due to temperature difference: heat.

## Main Rule

In physics, `heat` is energy transferred between objects because of a temperature difference.

Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is. Heat flows from hotter objects to colder objects until they reach the same temperature.

Basic formula for heat transfer:

`Q = mcΔT`

Read it aloud:

`Heat equals mass times specific heat capacity times change in temperature.`

This formula shows that heat depends on mass, material, and temperature change.

## 1. Temperature

### Brief Explanation

`Temperature` is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance.

When particles move faster, the temperature rises. When they move slower, the temperature falls. Temperature does not tell us about the total energy, only about the average energy per particle.

Temperature is measured in `degrees Celsius` (°C), `degrees Fahrenheit` (°F), or `kelvins` (K). In physics, we often use Celsius or kelvins.

Important: temperature is not energy itself. It is a measure of how energetic the particles are on average.

### Visual: Temperature and Particle Motion

```
   Low Temperature          High Temperature
   ┌─────────────┐          ┌─────────────┐
   │ •   •   •   │          │ • → • ← •   │
   │   •   •     │          │   → • → •   │
   │ •   •   •   │          │ • ← • → •   │
   └─────────────┘          └─────────────┘
   Slow particles           Fast particles
   Low KE average           High KE average
```

### Examples

1. Temperature measures how hot or cold something is.
2. Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
3. The temperature of the room is 22 degrees Celsius.
4. Temperature is measured with a thermometer.

## 2. Heat

### Brief Explanation

`Heat` is energy transferred from one object to another because of a temperature difference.

Heat always flows from a hotter object to a colder object. If you touch a hot cup, heat flows from the cup to your hand. If you touch ice, heat flows from your hand to the ice.

Heat is not something an object "has." An object has thermal energy. Heat is the energy in transit.

Heat is measured in `joules` (J), like other forms of energy.

### Visual: Heat Flow

```
   Hot Object (T1)          Cold Object (T2)
   ┌─────────────┐          ┌─────────────┐
   │  High T     │          │  Low T      │
   │  High KE    │ ────►    │  Low KE     │
   │  particles  │  Heat    │  particles  │
   └─────────────┘  Flow    └─────────────┘

   Heat flows: Hot → Cold
   Until: T1 = T2 (thermal equilibrium)
```

### Examples

1. Heat flows from hot to cold.
2. The stove transfers heat to the pot.
3. Heat is measured in joules.
4. Insulation reduces heat loss.

## 3. Thermal Energy

### Brief Explanation

`Thermal energy` is the total internal energy of a system due to the motion and arrangement of its particles.

It includes the kinetic energy of all moving particles and the potential energy between them. A hot object has more thermal energy than a cold object of the same mass and material.

Thermal energy is different from heat. An object can have a lot of thermal energy but not transfer heat if it is in thermal equilibrium with its surroundings.

### Visual: Thermal Energy

```
   Thermal Energy = Total KE of all particles

   System:
   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ • → • ← • → • ← •    │
   │   ← • → • ← • → •   │
   │ • → • ← • → • ← •    │
   └───────────────────────┘
   Sum of all particle KE = Thermal Energy
```

### Examples

1. A hot cup of coffee has high thermal energy.
2. Thermal energy depends on mass and temperature.
3. Thermal energy can change when heat is added or removed.
4. The system stores thermal energy until it is transferred.

## 4. Internal Energy

### Brief Explanation

`Internal energy` is the total energy inside a system. It includes kinetic energy of particles, potential energy between particles, and other forms of energy at the microscopic level.

In many cases, internal energy is mostly thermal energy. But internal energy can also include chemical energy, nuclear energy, and other forms.

When we add heat to a system, we increase its internal energy. When we do work on a system, we also change its internal energy.

### Visual: Internal Energy Components

```
   Internal Energy
   ├── Kinetic Energy of particles (thermal)
   ├── Potential Energy between particles
   ├── Chemical Energy
   └── Nuclear Energy
```

### Examples

1. Internal energy is the total energy inside a system.
2. Adding heat increases internal energy.
3. Doing work can change internal energy.
4. Internal energy depends on temperature, mass, and state.

## 5. Heat Transfer

### Brief Explanation

Heat can move in three ways: `conduction`, `convection`, and `radiation`.

`Conduction` is heat transfer through direct contact. When you touch a hot pan, heat moves from the pan to your hand through conduction.

`Convection` is heat transfer through the movement of fluids (liquids or gases). Hot air rises because it becomes less dense. This creates convection currents.

`Radiation` is heat transfer through electromagnetic waves. The Sun heats Earth through radiation. No medium is needed.

### Visual: Conduction

```
   Hot End          Cold End
   ┌──────┐        ┌──────┐
   │ High │ ─────► │ Low  │
   │  T   │  Heat  │  T   │
   └──────┘  Flow  └──────┘
   Direct contact, no movement of material
```

### Visual: Convection

```
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │ Hot fluid rises ↑   │
   │                     │
   │ ← ← ← ← ← ← ← ←  │
   │                     │
   │ Cold fluid sinks ↓  │
   └─────────────────────┘
   Convection current
```

### Visual: Radiation

```
   Sun ───────► Earth
        Heat via electromagnetic waves
        No medium needed
```

### Examples

1. Conduction transfers heat through direct contact.
2. Convection transfers heat through fluid movement.
3. Radiation transfers heat through electromagnetic waves.
4. Metals are good conductors of heat.

## 6. Specific Heat Capacity

### Brief Explanation

`Specific heat capacity` tells us how much heat is needed to raise the temperature of 1 kilogram of a substance by 1 degree Celsius.

Different materials have different specific heat capacities. Water has a high specific heat capacity, which means it takes a lot of heat to warm up water. Metals have low specific heat capacity, so they heat up quickly.

The formula:

`Q = mcΔT`

- `Q` is heat added (in joules)
- `m` is mass (in kilograms)
- `c` is specific heat capacity (in J/kg°C)
- `ΔT` is change in temperature (in °C)

### Visual: Specific Heat Capacity

```
   Water (high c)         Metal (low c)
   ┌─────────────┐        ┌─────────────┐
   │ Needs lots  │        │ Needs little│
   │ of heat to  │        │ heat to     │
   │ change T    │        │ change T    │
   └─────────────┘        └─────────────┘

   Example: Heat 1 kg by 1°C
   Water: Q = 4200 J
   Metal: Q = 500 J
```

### Visual: Q = mcΔT

```
   Heat (Q) = Mass (m) × Specific Heat (c) × ΔT

   Q ↑ when:
   • m ↑ (more mass)
   • c ↑ (higher specific heat)
   • ΔT ↑ (larger temperature change)
```

### Examples

1. Water has a high specific heat capacity.
2. Specific heat capacity depends on the material.
3. A high specific heat capacity means more heat is needed.
4. The specific heat capacity of water is about 4200 J/kg°C.

## How The Ideas Connect

- `temperature` measures average particle energy.
- `heat` is energy transferred due to temperature difference.
- `thermal energy` is total internal energy of a system.
- `heat transfer` happens by conduction, convection, or radiation.
- `specific heat capacity` tells us how much heat changes temperature.

### Visual: Connection Chain

```
Temperature Difference → Heat Flow → Thermal Energy Change → Temperature Change
        │                    │                │                      │
        ▼                    ▼                ▼                      ▼
   ΔT = T1 - T2      Q = heat transfer   ΔU = Q (if no work)   ΔT = Q/(mc)
```

### Visual: Heat Transfer Methods

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│           Heat Transfer            │
├────────────┬────────────┬───────────┤
│ Conduction │ Convection │ Radiation │
├────────────┼────────────┼───────────┤
│ Direct     │ Fluid      │ EM waves  │
│ contact    │ movement   │ No medium │
│ Metals     │ Water, air │ Sun heat  │
└────────────┴────────────┴───────────┘
```

## Common Confusion

### Temperature vs Heat

`Temperature` is a measure of how hot or cold something is. `Heat` is energy transferred because of temperature difference.

### Visual: Temperature vs Heat

```
   Temperature: Property of object
   ┌─────────────┐
   │ T = 50°C    │
   └─────────────┘

   Heat: Energy in transit
   ┌─────────────┐
   │ Q = 100 J   │
   │ (transferred)│
   └─────────────┘
```

Examples:

1. Everyday English: `It's hot outside.` (refers to temperature)
2. Physics English: `Heat flows from the sun to the earth.` (refers to energy transfer)
3. A block of metal at 50°C has high temperature, but not much heat unless it transfers energy to something cooler.

### Thermal Energy vs Internal Energy

`Thermal energy` is mostly the kinetic energy of particles. `Internal energy` includes thermal energy plus other forms of energy at the microscopic level.

### Visual: Thermal vs Internal Energy

```
   Internal Energy = Thermal Energy + Other Energy
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Internal Energy                        │
   │ ├── Thermal Energy (KE of particles)   │
   │ ├── Chemical Energy                    │
   │ └── Nuclear Energy                     │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Examples:

1. Internal energy includes thermal energy and other forms.
2. In many cases, internal energy is mostly thermal energy.
3. The terms are sometimes used interchangeably in simple contexts.

## Physics Calculation Practice

Use this formula:

`Q = mcΔT`

Read it aloud:

`Heat equals mass times specific heat capacity times change in temperature.`

Specific heat capacity of water: `c = 4200 J/kg°C`

### Visual: Heat Calculation

```
Given: m = 2 kg, c = 4200 J/kg°C, ΔT = 10°C
Formula: Q = mcΔT
Calculation: Q = 2 × 4200 × 10 = 84000 J
Answer: The heat added is 84000 J.
```

### Exercise 1

Calculate the heat added.

Answer pattern:

### Exercise 2

A metal block of mass `0.5 kg` receives `5000 J` of heat. The specific heat capacity of the metal is `500 J/kg°C`.

Calculate the temperature increase.

Answer pattern:

`The temperature increases by ... °C.`

### Visual: Temperature Change Calculation

```
Given: Q = 5000 J, m = 0.5 kg, c = 500 J/kg°C
Formula: ΔT = Q/(mc)
Calculation: ΔT = 5000/(0.5 × 500) = 20°C
Answer: The temperature increases by 20°C.
```
