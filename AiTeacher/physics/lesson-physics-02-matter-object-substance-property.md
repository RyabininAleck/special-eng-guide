# Physics Vocabulary Lesson 2

## Topic

Matter, object, substance, and property

## Goal

In this lesson, you learn four basic words that help you describe physical things in English. These words are simple, but they are important for reading physics texts, describing experiments, and explaining what materials are made of.

## Main Rule

In physics, we often describe a thing with this pattern:

`object -> substance -> properties`

For example:

- `The object is a metal cube.`
- `The substance is aluminum.`
- `Its properties include low density and good thermal conductivity.`

An `object` is one specific thing. A `substance` is the material it is made of. A `property` is a feature we can observe or measure.

## 1. Matter

### Brief Explanation

`Matter` is anything that has mass and takes up space.

This definition has two important parts. First, matter has `mass`, so it contains some amount of physical material. Second, matter takes up `space`, so it has volume. If something has no mass and takes up no space, we usually do not call it matter in basic physics.

In physics, matter includes solids, liquids, and gases. A stone, water, air, plastic, and metal are all forms of matter because they have mass and volume. Air is a useful example: you cannot see it easily, but it still has mass, occupies space, and can produce pressure. This helps you understand that matter is not only what is hard or visible.

When you speak about matter in English, use it as an uncountable noun. Say `matter is`, not `matters are`, when you mean physical material.

### Examples

1. Matter has mass and occupies space.
2. Solids, liquids, and gases are different states of matter.
3. Air is matter, even though we cannot see it.
4. Physics studies how matter behaves under different conditions.

## 2. Object

### Brief Explanation

An `object` is a particular thing that we can describe, measure, or study.

An object has boundaries in our thinking. We choose one thing and study it as a separate physical system. For example, if you study a falling ball, the ball is the object. If you study a drone, the drone is the object. If you study one sensor inside the drone, then the sensor becomes the object.

In physics, an object can be small or large: a ball, a metal block, a robot part, a satellite, or a car. We often study how an object moves, changes temperature, or interacts with other objects. The word is useful because it lets you speak clearly before you know the exact material or internal structure.

In scientific English, `object` is a neutral word. It does not mean something special or mysterious. It simply means the thing we are paying attention to.

### Examples

1. The object moved across the table.
2. We measured the mass of the object.
3. A sensor can detect the position of an object.
4. In this experiment, the object is a small metal sphere.

## 3. Substance

### Brief Explanation

A `substance` is a type of matter with a specific composition.

A substance answers the question: `What is it made of?` The object is the thing; the substance is the material. For example, a wire is an object, but copper is a substance. A bottle is an object, but glass or plastic is the substance.

Examples of substances are water, iron, oxygen, copper, glass, and plastic. When we ask "What is this made of?", we are usually asking about the substance. In physics and chemistry, this word is important because different substances behave differently under the same conditions.

For example, copper conducts electricity well, but rubber does not. Water freezes at a different temperature than alcohol. These differences come from the properties of the substances.

### Examples

1. Water is a common substance in physics experiments.
2. Copper is a conductive substance.
3. The substance changes from liquid to gas when heated.
4. Different substances have different densities.

## 4. Property

### Brief Explanation

A `property` is a characteristic of matter, an object, or a substance.

Properties help us describe and compare things. If two objects look similar, their properties may still be different. For example, two cubes may have the same size, but one may be made of plastic and the other of steel. Their mass, density, and electrical conductivity will be different.

Some properties can be measured, such as mass, volume, density, temperature, and electrical resistance. Other properties can be observed, such as color, shape, or state. In physics, measured properties are especially important because they allow us to make precise comparisons.

The word `property` is common in academic and technical English. Engineers talk about material properties, electrical properties, thermal properties, and mechanical properties. When you learn this word well, you can describe systems more professionally.

### Examples

1. Density is an important property of a material.
2. Color is a visible property of an object.
3. Electrical conductivity is a useful property in engineering.
4. The properties of a substance can change with temperature.

## How The Ideas Connect

- `matter` is the general physical stuff of the universe.
- an `object` is one specific thing made of matter.
- a `substance` is the material the object is made of.
- a `property` describes what the object or substance is like.

Simple example:

- `object`: a wire
- `substance`: copper
- `properties`: flexible, conductive, reddish-brown, dense

## Useful Language Patterns

These sentence patterns are useful in academic and technical English:

1. `This object is made of ...`
2. `The substance has several important properties.`
3. `One property of this material is ...`
4. `Matter can exist as a solid, liquid, or gas.`
5. `We measured the physical properties of the sample.`

## Common Confusion

### Object vs Substance

An `object` is a thing. A `substance` is what the thing is made of.

Examples:

1. A spoon is an object. Steel is the substance.
2. A bottle is an object. Glass or plastic is the substance.
3. A wire is an object. Copper is often the substance.

### Property vs Quantity

A `property` is a characteristic. A `quantity` is a measurable value.

Examples:

1. `Density` is a property.
2. `7.8 g/cm^3` is a measured value of density.
3. `Temperature` is a property.
4. `25 degrees Celsius` is a measured value of temperature.

## Physics Calculation Practice

This lesson is mostly conceptual, but you can still practice calculating and comparing physical properties.

Use the formula:

`density = mass / volume`

Read the formula aloud:

`Density equals mass divided by volume.`

### Exercise 1

Object A is made of plastic.

- mass: `60 g`
- volume: `30 cm^3`

Calculate the density.

Answer pattern:

`Object A has a density of ... g/cm^3.`

### Exercise 2

Object B is made of metal.

- mass: `180 g`
- volume: `30 cm^3`

Calculate the density.

Then compare Object A and Object B:

`Object B is denser than Object A because ...`

### Exercise 3

Two objects have the same volume: `50 cm^3`.

- Object C has a mass of `100 g`.
- Object D has a mass of `250 g`.

Calculate the density of both objects.

Then answer:

`Which object is probably made of the denser substance?`
