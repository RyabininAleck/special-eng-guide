# Medical English: At the Doctor's Office

## Patients

* **a patient** — A person who is receiving medical care. You are a patient only while a doctor is treating you.
* **an inpatient** — A patient who stays at the hospital overnight — a night, a week, or even longer. They have a hospital room and a bed.
* **ICU (Intensive Care Unit)** — The part of the hospital where very sick inpatients get the highest level of care.
* **an outpatient** — A patient who gets medical care but does NOT stay at the hospital. They treat you, and then you leave.
* **ER (Emergency Room)** — The part of the hospital where you go with a sudden, serious problem. ER patients are outpatients.

## Medical Professionals

* **a doctor / a physician** — The same thing. In North America people usually say "doctor".
* **a GP (General Practitioner)** — A doctor who treats a wide range of health issues: back pain, a cold, an eye problem. Your "family doctor".
* **a surgeon** — A doctor who performs surgery (operations).
* **a specialist** — A doctor who focuses on one specific area of medicine.
* **a cardiologist** — Heart.
* **a dermatologist** — Skin.
* **a pediatrician** — Children.
* **an optometrist** — Eyes. If you wear glasses, you see them often.
* **a dentist** — Oral health, teeth.
* **an OB/GYN** — Women's health and pregnancy. Everybody just says "O-B-G-Y-N".
* **an anesthesiologist** — Gives anesthesia (makes you unconscious before surgery) and monitors you during surgery.
* **a radiologist** — Does X-rays, CT scans, and MRIs.
* **a psychiatrist** — Mind and mental disorders.
* **an ENT** — Ear, Nose, Throat. That's what the letters stand for.
* **a nurse** — Helps doctors and takes care of patients.
* **an RN (Registered Nurse)** — A nurse with a nursing degree and a license.
* **an NP (Nurse Practitioner)** — A nurse with advanced training. Can diagnose and treat certain medical conditions.

## The Visit

* **a routine check-up** — A regular visit to your GP (every 6–12 months) just to review your overall health. You don't have a specific problem.
* **vital signs** — The basic numbers your GP checks: temperature, blood pressure, heart rate, and respiratory rate.
* **an appointment** — A set time and date to see your doctor. You schedule (make) an appointment.
* **to refer** — When your GP sends you to a specialist. Example: "I'm going to refer you to an ENT."
* **a referral** — The document/order for that visit to a specialist.

## Symptoms

* **a symptom** — Any feeling of illness you have. The doctor's first question: "What are your symptoms?"
* **sore** — Painful when you touch or use it. Example: "My left arm is very sore."
* **numb** — You can't feel this part of your body at all. Example: "My feet are numb."
* **blurry** — You can't see well; everything is unclear. Example: "My vision is blurry."
* **nauseous** — The feeling that you are going to vomit (like on a roller coaster). Example: "I feel nauseous."

## Diagnosis and Treatment

* **diagnostic tests** — Tests to find out if a disease or condition is present or absent. Examples: X-ray, MRI, CT scan, blood test.
* **a diagnosis** — The doctor's judgment about what illness or problem you have. (Verb: to diagnose.)
* **treatment options** — The different courses of action you can take to address the medical issue.

## Prescriptions and Pharmacy

* **a prescription** — A written or electronic order from a doctor for a specific medication.
* **a pharmacy** — The shop where you get your medication.
* **a pharmacist** — The person at the pharmacy who fills your prescription.
* **to fill a prescription** — To prepare and give you the medication from the prescription.
* **a dosage** — The amount of medication you take (how much, how often).
* **side effects** — Unintended reactions of a medication. Example: the pill treats your arm but gives you a headache.
* **a follow-up appointment** — A later appointment to check if the treatment is working and if the dosage needs changes.

### 1. Fill in the gaps

1. The doctor wrote me a ______ for antibiotics.
2. You can get your prescription filled at a ______.
3. The ______ prepares your medication.
4. Take two pills twice a day — that's your ______.
5. This pill can cause headaches. That's a common ______ effect.
6. Come back in two weeks for a ______ appointment — we'll check if the treatment works.

### 2. True or false?

1. A pharmacist writes prescriptions.
2. "To fill a prescription" means to give you the medication.
3. Side effects are the intended effects of a drug.
4. A follow-up appointment happens before the first appointment.
5. Dosage tells you how much medication to take.

#### Answers

**1. Fill in the gaps:** 1. prescription 2. pharmacy 3. pharmacist 4. dosage 5. side 6. follow-up

**2. True or false:** 1. False (a doctor/NP writes them) 2. True 3. False (they are unintended) 4. False (it comes after) 5. True

### 9. Role-play — samples

**Situation 1 (calling a clinic):**

— Hi, I'd like to schedule an appointment with a GP.
— What are your symptoms?
— My arm is very sore, my feet are numb, and I feel nauseous.
— Okay, the doctor will see you on Tuesday.

**Situation 2 (routine check-up):**

— How do you feel?
— Fine. It's just a routine check-up.
— Good. Let me check your vital signs: temperature, blood pressure, heart rate.

**Situation 3 (pharmacy):**

— Hi, I'd like to get my prescription filled.
— Sure. The dosage is one pill twice a day.
— Are there any side effects?
— It may cause headaches.

**Situation 4 (follow-up):**

— How are your symptoms?
— Much better. My arm isn't sore anymore.
— Great. We'll keep the same dosage. Come back in a month.

### 10. Mini-story — sample

She wrote me a prescription, and the pharmacist at the pharmacy filled it. She explained the dosage and the possible side effects. In two weeks I have a follow-up appointment to see if the treatment is working.
